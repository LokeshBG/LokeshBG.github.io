Workday · 2024–2026 · Finance operations

Flux Capacitor

An ML engine that finds the account movements worth explaining at month-end close, traces them to the journal lines behind them, and drafts the explanation.

The problem

Every month, balances move in hundreds of accounts and finance has to explain the important changes. The old way was a spreadsheet export and a fixed rule, such as anything over 10%. Seasonal accounts tripped that rule every cycle, large accounts hid material dollar swings under it, and analysts spent three to four days per account searching journal entries for the cause.

Stop explaining every variance. Explain the ones that matter.

How it works

  1. 01 Detect Which movements deserve attention? One forecasting model per account and flux category learns its trend and seasonality. A balance outside the 80% prediction interval is flagged. LightGBM, SARIMA and Holt-Winters, chosen per series in H2O.ai
  2. 02 Decode Which transactions caused them? Engineered features on every journal line feed three outlier models. A line surfaces only when at least two agree, and SHAP shows why. One-class SVM, local outlier factor, isolation forest
  3. 03 Diagnose What should the analyst say? An LLM drafts the commentary from those findings. Every figure comes from the pipeline, and nothing is final until an analyst approves it. Scored against a gold-standard set for accuracy and completeness
  1. Workday ledger and journal data, by API
  2. Bronze
  3. Silver
  4. Gold Delta tables
  5. Month-end Databricks job
  6. Results tables
  7. Stakeholder dashboard

What changed at month end

< 2 hours per account for variance analysis, down from 3–4 days
170+ accounts covered, each with 5–15 flux categories
80% of flux explained per account on average, over six months
~50% less manual analysis effort for Treasury, Tax and AP/AR

I built the data pipeline, models, LLM layer and dashboard myself, with a sponsor in Tax as business partner. It started as a pilot on sales tax accounts, then rolled out to payroll, accounting and treasury. Within a year it covered every account in the finance function.

Three decisions that made it work

What I hitWhat I did
ForecastingOne global model across every account and category gave poor results. One model per account and flux category, validated walk-forward on a 48-month train and 12-month test window.
AnomaliesA single outlier detector was too much trust to place in one model. Three detectors vote. A journal line counts as an outlier only if flagged two times out of three as contamination varies from 1 to 10%.
LanguageFinance numbers have to be auditable, and an LLM cannot be their source. The LLM writes the words only. Every figure comes from the pipeline and reconciles to the ledger.
Validation and monitoring
  • Walk-forward cross-validation: train on 48 months, test on the next 12, roll forward one month and repeat. MAE, RMSE and MAPE are averaged across folds, and the lowest average error wins for each account and category.
  • Before each run, input checks confirm every expected account and flux category has arrived, so none are silently missed.
  • Each model's accuracy is tracked month over month to catch drift, and models are retrained before performance drops.
Why finance trusts it
  • Accuracy. Numbers come from source systems, never from the LLM.
  • Accountability. Every explanation needs analyst approval.
  • Auditability. Inputs, prompts, model versions and outputs are logged.
How I would rebuild it today with AI agents
StepWith agents

Intel · 2022–2024 · Semiconductor manufacturing

SEM defect classification

A PyTorch computer vision model that labels scanning electron microscope images of wafer defects, so engineers can stop sorting images and start on root causes.

The problem

A fab finds defects in three steps. An inspection tool scans the wafer and marks where the defects are. A review SEM photographs each one. Then someone decides what type of defect each photo shows, because the type points to the cause: particles suggest a contaminated tool, scratches suggest handling. The first two steps were automated. The third was done by eye. It was slow, two engineers could label the same image differently, and the only way to go faster was to add people.

Detecting a defect says something went wrong. Classifying it says where to look.

One step in the loop changed

  1. 1 Process step Layers are built on the wafer.
  2. 2 Inspection A scan flags defect locations.
  3. 3 SEM review A close-up is taken of each defect.
  4. 4 Classification Before Engineers label each image by eye. After The model labels each image as 1 of 8 classes.
  5. 5 Engineering action Root cause and process fix.

Design choices driven by scarce, varied data

ChallengeApproach
Few labeled defect imagesSegment each defect and paste it onto new backgrounds to create synthetic training images.
Varied imaging conditionsAugment the segmented defects before compositing.
Synthetic data can flatter resultsHold out 10% of the real images and test only on those.
Deployment near fab toolsSqueezeNet, a lightweight CNN with few parameters.
Hyperparameter choiceGrid search over optimizer (SGD, Adam) and learning rate.
Eight uneven classesScore with F1 and Cohen's kappa, since accuracy alone hides weak classes.

Result

95% classification accuracy
8 classes no defect, plus seven defect types
Real images only in the test set, so the score is never earned on synthetic data
Training setup
  • Stage 1, data. Resize and crop the review SEM images. Segment each defect, augment it, and paste it onto new backgrounds. Split the synthetic set 80/20 into train and validation.
  • Stage 2, model. Grid search over SGD and Adam with learning rates from 10−1 to 10−6, batch size 20 and cross-entropy loss. Train SqueezeNet, predict on the real test set, and evaluate with F1, Cohen's kappa and validation loss.

Gracenote · 2019–2022 · Media and streaming

Personalized Imagery

A multi-stage computer vision pipeline that turns trailers into ranked, editor-ready artwork, so a streaming service can show each viewer the image most likely to interest them.

The problem

Personalized artwork needs several strong images for every title, and editors were curating them by hand at about 45 minutes a title. Sourcing stills from production houses works for blockbusters. It breaks down for older, indie and niche titles, which is most of a catalog.

Every title has a trailer, and a studio has already picked its best two minutes.

Trailers show a film's most striking moments without major spoilers, and processing one takes a fraction of the compute and storage of a two-hour feature.

The curation pipeline

  1. Source the trailer
  2. Decode frames
  3. Detect shot boundaries
  4. Run vision models
  5. Score each frame
  6. Rank frames
  7. Shortlist for the editor
  • Face present At least one recognizable face is detected.
  • No intertitles Text detection excludes title cards and overlays.
  • High quality A blind image quality score keeps crisp frames.
  • No motion blur A custom classifier rejects blurred frames.

The model I trained

Shot detection and image quality ran on open-source models. Motion blur needed a custom one. I built the dataset in-house, had it labeled on Amazon Mechanical Turk, compared InceptionV3 and DenseNet121 in PyTorch, and chose DenseNet for its faster inference. Augmentation and partial layer unfreezing did not help. Retraining all layers did, reaching 91.6% test accuracy and a 0.91 F1 score.

Result

+11.2% hours watched per visitor
+7.7% titles watched per visitor
20–25 min editor curation time per title, down from 45

Consumption lift measured in a six-month A/B test by a top streaming service across 1,000 titles. Built by a team of two data scientists and one engineer.

How it scales
  • An ingestion service pushes video IDs to an SQS queue. Failed jobs go to a second queue for retry.
  • CPU worker pods on Kubernetes (EKS) pull jobs, decode frames with OpenCV, detect shot boundaries, then run face, text, quality and blur inference concurrently.
  • KEDA scales the pod count with queue depth. Processed frames and metadata land in S3, and Terraform manages the infrastructure.
Five things that broke, and the fixes
ChallengeFix
Letterboxed trailers distorted the aspect ratio and degraded frame quality.Detect and crop the black bars with FFmpeg before any other processing.
Face detectors trained on real faces were inconsistent on animated content.Identify heavily stylized trailers and exclude them from the pipeline.
Shot boundary models expect a whole video, which does not scale.Run detection on 5-second sliding windows with contextual chunking.
Processing frames one at a time made inference slow.Parallelize inference across frames.
A fixed pod count could not absorb variable load.Kubernetes autoscaling, so pods follow demand.
